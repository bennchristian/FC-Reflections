## Gap questions

Answer these in your own words. Rough notes are fine and can be shaped into paragraphs later.

### Section 2: What I did (~160 words)
1. What did a typical week of the Fellowship look like? What were the regular rhythms (sessions, check-ins, gatherings)?
2. Your cover letter says FC gave you "hands-on experience building with these tools." What did you actually build or work on? Who was it for?
3. You mentioned *mentorship, discipleship, community*. What did each one look like in practice? Is there a person or a conversation that stands out?
4. "What we had to do": was there a team project, a challenge or a deadline you all worked toward?

### Section 3: What I learnt (~130 words)
1. What do you know now that you didn't two months ago? It could be about AI tools, innovation, or how you work.
2. Was there a moment when something clicked, or when something went wrong and taught you a lesson?
3. Did anything shift in how you think about faith, calling or your work as a lawyer? *(Only if you want this in the post.)*
4. If you could keep only one lesson, which would it be?

### Section 4: How I grew (~130 words)
1. Where did the confidence show up? Describe a specific moment when you did something you wouldn't have done before FC.
2. You listed "what I had to struggle with". What was hard, and how did you get through it?
3. Complete this: "Before FC, I would have ___. Now I ___."
4. Your cover letter offers to contribute to the firm's business operations beyond legal work. Would you have felt able to offer that before FC? If not, this could be your proof of growing in confidence, and it leads straight into Section 5.

## Small notes on the existing text (optional)

- **The hook's punchline can be read two ways.** "I believe that it was in fact too good to be true!" could sound like it *didn't* live up to the promise. If you mean "it really was that good," consider "…it was in fact as good as it sounded, and it was true!" or let ending option 1 make the meaning clear.
- **British spelling:** "recognize" → "recognise", to match "organisation".
- **"Up to me, I'd stay on…"** reads more smoothly as "If it were up to me, I'd stay on…"
