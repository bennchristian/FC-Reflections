# Blog Post Structure: My Time at FC

A fill-in outline for a 500–750 word reflective blog post. Everything in `[BRACKETS]` is yours to write; nothing here is real content yet.

**Target length:** ~650 words (anywhere from 500 to 750 works)
**Shape:** setup → what happened → turning point → change → payoff. It opens on one moment and closes by coming back to it.
**Shared with the video:** these five sections match the five beats of the reflection video, so one set of answers can feed both.

---

## Title options

Pick one, or use them as a starting point:

1. `[Number] [months/weeks] at FC: What I Didn't Expect`
2. `What FC Taught Me About [THEME]`
3. `Before and After FC`
4. `The [OBJECT / MOMENT] That Summed Up My Time at FC`
5. `Why I'd Say Yes to FC Again`

---

## Opening hook options

The first 1–3 sentences of Section 1. Pick one pattern and fill it in.

| Pattern | Template |
|---|---|
| **A moment** | `It was [TIME/PLACE] and I was [DOING WHAT] when I realised [WHAT].` |
| **A contrast** | `When I started at FC I thought [OLD BELIEF]. By the end, [NEW BELIEF].` |
| **A question** | `[QUESTION YOU ASKED YOURSELF BEFORE JOINING]? I didn't have an answer when I started.` |
| **A detail** | `[ONE SMALL, CONCRETE THING: an object, a phrase, a sound] is the thing I'll remember most about FC.` |
| **A confession** | `I almost didn't [JOIN / APPLY / STAY]. [WHY].` |

Whichever you pick, the closing returns to it (see **Closing pattern** below).

## Section 1: Why I joined

**Heading:** *Why I Said Yes*
**Alternatives:** *Where It Started* · *Before FC*

**Word budget:** ~120 (range 90–140) · **Running total: 120**

**Job:** Show who you were and what you wanted before FC, so the reader can see the change later.

**Prompts:**
- What was going on in your life or work just before you joined?
- What made you say yes? Was it a person, a moment, or a goal?
- What did you hope to get out of it, and what were you unsure about?
- *(If using the hook)* What moment or detail captures how you felt at the start?

## Section 2: What I did

**Heading:** *What the Work Looked Like*
**Alternatives:** *Inside FC* · *The Day-to-Day*

**Word budget:** ~160 (range 120–180) · **Running total: 280**

**Job:** Give the reader a concrete picture of your role and one or two things you actually worked on. This is the longest section because everything after it depends on it.

**Prompts:**
- What was your role, in one plain sentence someone outside FC would understand?
- What are one or two projects, tasks or moments that best represent your time there?
- What did a typical day or week involve?
- Who did you work with or for, and what difference did the work make for them?

> **[YOUR STORY HERE: what you did]**
>
> *Tip: one specific scene (a meeting, an event, a deadline) does more than a list of duties. Name real numbers and details only if they're accurate.*

**Hand-off:** Pick the moment from this section that taught you the most. Section 3 starts from it.

## Section 3: What I learnt

**Heading:** *What I Learnt*
**Alternatives:** *The Lessons* · *What Surprised Me*

**Word budget:** ~130 (range 100–150) · **Running total: 410**

**Job:** The turning point. Name the one or two insights FC gave you and the moment each one landed.

**Prompts:**
- What do you know now that you didn't before FC? Think skills, ideas, or how things work.
- Was there a moment where something clicked, or where you got something wrong and it taught you?
- What surprised you most?
- If you could keep only one lesson, which would it be?

> **[YOUR STORY HERE: what you learnt]**
>
> *Tip: two lessons with a moment behind each beat five lessons stated in a list.*

**Hand-off:** Sections 3 and 4 can blur together, so keep them apart: *learnt* is what you now know, *grew* is how you've changed. Close this section by asking yourself "and what did that change about me?"

---

## Section 4: How I grew

**Heading:** *How I've Changed*
**Alternatives:** *Who I Am Now* · *The Difference*

**Word budget:** ~130 (range 100–150) · **Running total: 540**

**Job:** Show the transformation as a before and after in how you think, act or see yourself.

**Prompts:**
- What do you do differently now: at work, with people, or under pressure?
- What did you used to avoid or doubt that you now handle with confidence?
- Has anyone noticed the change? What did they say?
- Complete this: "Before FC I was ___; now I'm ___."

> **[YOUR STORY HERE: how you grew]**
>
> *Tip: show it with something you now do rather than saying "I became more confident."*

**Hand-off:** Turn from looking back to looking forward: "here's who I am now, so here's what comes next."

## Section 5: What I'll take into the future

**Heading:** *What I'm Taking With Me*
**Alternatives:** *What Comes Next* · *Carrying It Forward*

**Word budget:** ~110 (range 90–130) · **Running total: 650**

**Job:** Connect the experience to what comes next, then close the loop back to the opening hook.

**Prompts:**
- What's next for you, and how will FC shape it?
- Which lesson or habit will you keep using?
- Is there someone you want to thank, or advice you'd give the next person joining FC?
- How would you answer your opening hook now?

## Closing pattern: return to the hook

The last 1–2 sentences pick up the opening image or question and show how it looks now.

| If you opened with… | Close with… |
|---|---|
| **A moment** | `Now when I [SIMILAR SITUATION], I [WHAT YOU DO DIFFERENTLY].` |
| **A contrast** | `I came in believing [OLD BELIEF]. I'm leaving knowing [NEW BELIEF].` |
| **A question** | `So, [REPEAT THE QUESTION]? [YOUR ANSWER NOW, in one line].` |
| **A detail** | `[THE SAME DETAIL] still [WHAT IT MEANS TO YOU NOW].` |
| **A confession** | `I'm glad I [JOINED / STAYED]. [ONE-LINE REASON].` |

## Tone and voice

- **First person, past tense** for the story; **present tense** for what you think now.
- **Reflective, not a CV.** Write about what the work meant to you and what it changed, not just a list of what you did.
- **Specific beats general.** One real name, place, number or quote you remember is worth more than any adjective. Only include details that are true and that you're comfortable sharing publicly; check before naming colleagues or the people you worked with.
- **Honest.** Doubts, mistakes and hard moments make the growth believable. You don't need to make everything sound positive.
- **Plain language.** Explain FC-specific terms the first time you use them, and decide whether to spell out "FC" for readers who don't know it.
- **One feeling to leave the reader with.** Decide what it is (gratitude, pride, excitement, something else) and let the closing land on it.
